## Findings repaired

1. **P1 — Coasting could defeat the reaction floor.** The sum of ship and rock speeds
   was used for arrival while placement used their actual velocity difference. A
   backward-coasting example placed a rock only 3.8 cm from the ship, inside a
   390 cm contact envelope. The repair uses actual relative motion and first
   surface contact. The compound hull's conservative, rotation-invariant bounds
   replace the old 105 cm sphere assumption for admission.
2. **P1 — Intended misses could hit.** Offsets in the heading's Right/Up plane did
   not remain perpendicular to relative travel during sideways coasting. A 540 cm
   nominal miss could pass within 53.7 cm. Offsets now use the relative-motion
   plane, and misses clear the complete hull envelope.
3. **P2 — Stale future arrivals suppressed attacks.** One scalar timestamp survived
   destruction and encounter reset; a 120 cm/s rock could reserve an 80-second
   arrival despite its 65-second lifetime. Admission now rejects shots that
   expire before passing, and reservations belong to live actors. Full possible
   surface-contact intervals are spaced on both sides; reset and destruction
   release them. `ss.HazardDirectShare` is a candidate probability, not a promise
   that exactly 60% of admitted rocks hit after spacing and clearance checks.

No changes were made to the villain presentation, damage numbers, controls,
world asteroid field, station, assets or release payload.
