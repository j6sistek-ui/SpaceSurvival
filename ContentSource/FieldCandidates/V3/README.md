# Field V3: soft extent and broad coverage

V1 and V2 remain preserved. V1 native review rejected its opaque cage; V2 reduced the interior obstruction but its exterior strands broke into dots and its boundary was almost invisible. Lead approved this separate V3 approach. V3 has now been imported, fresh-validated and captured in eight native static views. The lead approved V3 for integrated gameplay evaluation after these native previews; the selection bridge is now in source and awaits the combined build. Owner visual acceptance remains open.

The three great-circle wires are replaced by a spherical additive boundary surface with smooth radial normals and a subdued Fresnel rim. Its interior opacity is low; it is not an opaque body, central core or collision surface. Maximum radius and axis extents are exactly 100 cm. After the existing runtime normalization, the visual outer envelope still matches BodyRadius.

Internal light paths use two orthogonal crossed ribbons, each with three vertices across its width. Broad support geometry provides pixel coverage; a narrower shader core uses fwidth-based filtering with energy scaling. A soft Gaussian halo fades to zero at the ribbon edge, and the two planes are weighted by view orientation. Electrical primary half-width is 2.3 cm, branch half-width 1.5 cm and gravity half-width 2.6 cm at the 100 cm source radius. These are support widths for mostly transparent light, not solid pipes. Gravity samples increase from 45 to 65 while the number of curves falls from 9 to 6.

The dedicated material remains one additive/unlit/depth-tested section. Two-sided rendering permits boundary and ribbon inspection from inside. There are no texture assets, refraction, WPO, depth-test disabling or global AA/bloom changes. Exact Tint, Color and Emission parameters remain. UV0.x is role*4+longitudinal fraction; UV0.y is strand*4+cross-ribbon width 0..1. Boundary triangles use role 0; main/branch ribbons use roles 1/2. PixelNormalWS and CameraVectorWS provide orientation, while fwidth filters the ribbon core in the pixel shader.

Electrical grain is bounded decorative motion. The white discharge accent derives solely from supplied Emission above 2.1, preserving the existing charge and 2.8 discharge clock; no independent pulse schedule exists. Gravity uses six smooth outer-to-inner curves and bounded decorative flow; supplied Emission still controls amplitude. The spherical boundary has no Time-driven fade. A positive mathematical opacity does not establish adequate gameplay visibility.
